# Literature evidence for the 2026-11 re-engineering

Collected on 2026-11-08 by a research run for the system audit. **How each source
was read is marked:** [FT] full text, [Abs] abstract only, [Snip] search snippet
only. PubMed pages were not readable (cookie challenge); PMC full texts were read
through PMC's official OAI-PMH API (open-access articles only). Nothing was read
from unofficial copies. Numbers below are quoted from the sources as read; where
a statement is the researcher's own arithmetic it is marked as such.

## Q1 Determinants of attractiveness; additive vs configural
- Rhodes 2006, Annu Rev Psychol, doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.57.102904.190208 [Snip] — averageness, symmetry, sexual dimorphism attractive across cultures (no effect sizes obtained).
- Pallett, Link & Lee 2010, Vision Res, PMC2814183 [FT] — length ratio (eye–mouth ÷ hairline–chin) optimum .36–.37 (SD .017 in 40 white female faces); width ratio (interpupillary ÷ inner ear edges) optimum .46 (SD .022); curvilinear fits R² .83/.69/.68 (length), .48 (width); golden-ratio values (.38) significantly different from the preferred values; white female faces only.
- Foo, Simmons & Rhodes 2017, Sci Rep, PMC5290736 [FT] — men: masculinity β .35, averageness .28, symmetry .30, adiposity −.42; women: femininity .70, adiposity −.21, averageness/symmetry n.s. (rated traits, not geometry).
- Lee et al. 2025, Sci Rep, PMC11828934 [FT] — 132 landmarks, 100 faces, 400 raters: averageness predicted attractiveness in both sexes; symmetry and male masculinity did not.
- Kleisner et al. 2024, Evol Hum Behav, doi:10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2023.10.001 [Snip] — 1,550 faces, 10 populations: distinctiveness lowered attractiveness, symmetry n.s.
- Holzleitner et al. 2019, JEP:HPP, doi:10.1037/xhp0000685 [Snip] — single traits are poor predictors.
- Configural evidence: Abbas & Duchaine 2008 [Snip] (misaligned halves remove the influence of the lower half); Liu & Chen 2018, PMC6030301 [Abs]; Lin & Zhou 2021 [Snip] (interaction). Additive counter-example: Mogilski & Welling 2018, PMC6290027 [FT] (single-feature edits, small effects).
- Gao 2025, Behav Sci, PMC11851706 [FT] — whole-face attractiveness predicted by eyes (adj. R² .55), nose (.20), mouth (.12); forehead not predictive.
- **Gap:** no direct test of whether strong features compensate weak ones.

## Q2 FWHR, jaw, cheekbones
- Geniole et al. 2015 meta-analysis, PMC4504483 [FT] — higher FWHR judged less attractive, r̄ = −.26 (95 % CI −.40 to −.10, k = 14); FWHR ~ BMI r = .31; tiny sex difference (d = .11).
- Kramer, Jones & Ward 2012, PMC3413652 [FT] — 2D vs 3D FWHR r = .81; BMI r = .43/.52; pitch and yaw distort FWHR.
- Cunningham 1986, JPSP [Snip] — prominent cheekbones, narrow cheeks attractive (female faces).
- Scheib et al. 1999, PMC1690211 [Abs]; Mogilski & Welling 2018 [FT] — jaw prominence mattered most among tested features, direction sex-specific; cheekbone effects small (d ≈ .2).

## Q3 Population norms ≠ aesthetic ideals
- Fang, Clapham & Chung 2011, PMC3074358 [FT] — 27 groups, 2,359 people; within-group CV (women): intercanthal .087, nasal width .078, mouth width .076, biocular width .051, bizygomatic .046, forehead height .098; largest between-group differences: forehead height, interocular distance, nasal width.
- Farkas et al. 2005 [Snip]; Wen et al. 2015, PMC4527668 [Abs]; Weinberg 2019, PMC6571015 [FT] (3D vs caliper norms disagree: same fissure length +0.23 SD vs −1.30 SD).
- Jayaratne et al. 2012, PMC3532441 [FT] — neoclassical canons rarely hold (best 19 %).
- Zwahlen et al. 2022, PMC9452610 [Abs] — attractive faces deviated from "ideal" targets on 42 of 77 measures.

## Q4 Camera distance
- Ward et al. 2018, JAMA Facial Plast Surg, PMC5876805 [FT] (geometric model) — at 30 cm nasal width appears +30 % (men) / +29 % (women); negligible at 1.5 m.
- Třebický et al. 2016, PMC4760932 [FT] — FWHR −4.5 % (women) / −5.1 % (men) at 50 mm vs 85 mm equivalent framing.
- Healy & Stephan 2023, PMC10567895 [FT] — camera-distance estimation error 11 % (DSLR) / 26 % (smartphone).

## Q5 Head pose
- Battistoni et al. 2021, PMC8073202 [FT] — 3D mannequin: within 0.89 mm up to yaw 16°, roll/pitch 11°; 2D photos lack this invariance.
- Kramer 2012 [FT] — pupil alignment removes roll.
- No 2D tolerance study found. Researcher's arithmetic: widths shrink by cos(yaw) (−1.5 % at 10°); the nose tip (~21 mm anterior) shifts ~21·sin(yaw) mm (3.7 mm at 10°), i.e. asymmetry metrics are far more pose-sensitive than ratios.

## Q6 Landmark accuracy
- Lim et al. 2022, PMC8826070 [FT] — 2D photos vs calipers (96 people): 8/10 dimensions within 2 mm; **bigonial width bias 9.3 mm, inter-rater ICC .16**; bizygomatic bias 3.3 mm, ICC .66.
- Türkün et al. 2026, PMC13205844 [Abs] — trichion, menton, gonion with the highest errors.
- Kleisner et al. 2025 (FaceDig), PMC12234795 [FT] — MediaPipe "precision is insufficient" for anthropometric use; largest corrections vertical at trichion and gnathion.
- Er Bilir 2026, PMC13049709 [FT] — MediaPipe eyelid measures vs ophthalmologists ICC .93 (close-ups).

## Q7 Asymmetry
- Methods: mirror + Procrustes (Lee 2025, FaceDig), Procrustes ANOVA (Pound 2014, PMC4150332 [FT]: FA only 3.2× measurement-error variance; ICC .77), EDMA (Ferrario 1995 [Abs], Ercan 2008 [Abs]), mirror RMS (Patel 2015, PMC4614853 [FT]).
- Perception: chin deviation noticed at 4 mm by laypeople, 2 mm by specialists (Alrbata 2020, PMC7603600 [Abs]); −1.22 points per 2 mm (Zhang 2023, PMC10354989 [Abs]).
- Link to attractiveness weak and publication-biased (Van Dongen 2011 [Snip]); Hatch 2017, PMC5630144 [Abs].

## Q8 Combining noisy measurements
- Keogh et al. 2020 (STRATOS), PMC7450672 [FT] — attenuation λ = var(true)/(var(true)+var(error)); effects shrink by λ.
- Hutcheon 2010 [Snip] — regression dilution; Song 2013, PMC5459482 [FT]; Dawes 1979 [Snip] — equal/unit weights are robust.
- Mean of k independent z-scores has SD 1/√k (0.15 for 42 features); with mean correlation ρ̄ the variance is (1+(k−1)ρ̄)/k.

## What the evidence does not support
Golden ratio as an ideal; neoclassical canons or universal fixed ideal ratios; the population mean as the optimum for every ratio; symmetry as a major driver; wider faces / higher FWHR being more attractive; raw MediaPipe jaw and hairline landmarks as anthropometric-grade; a proven rule that features compensate — or that they don't.
